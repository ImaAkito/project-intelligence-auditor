# Commercialization Audit

Commercial analysis must not treat technical novelty as market demand.

Assess each applicable dimension independently on a 0–100 scale and record evidence/confidence:

- problem severity;
- target customer / ICP clarity;
- value proposition clarity;
- differentiation;
- technical moat;
- data moat;
- workflow moat;
- switching costs;
- deployment complexity;
- support burden;
- sales complexity;
- pricing potential;
- cost structure;
- unit-economics feasibility;
- market accessibility;
- regulatory/legal barriers;
- IP potential;
- time to first revenue;
- retention potential;
- scalability.

## Commercial readiness

Commercial Readiness should answer whether the project has a credible path from technology to a repeatable paid offering.

Do not raise Commercial Readiness merely because:

- the technology works;
- competitors exist;
- the market is large;
- a pricing page could be created;
- an LLM suggests that customers might pay.

Prefer concrete evidence such as interviews, pilots, LOIs, paying users, conversion data, deployment experience, buyer feedback, validated willingness to pay, or demonstrable cost savings.

## Gap analysis

Always separate:

### Technology → Product
What is required to move from developer/researcher operation to a usable product?

### Product → Commercial
What is required before a customer has a credible reason and mechanism to pay?

### Commercial → Scale
What is required to repeat sales, deployment, support, and delivery economically?

## Direction scoring

For alternative directions, consider:

- technical fit;
- reuse of current assets;
- additional development required;
- differentiation;
- defensibility;
- commercial complexity;
- risk;
- time to MVP;
- time to revenue;
- strategic value.

Do not force one winner if evidence does not distinguish the options. Mark close alternatives and specify what experiment would resolve the uncertainty.

## Adversarial questions

Answer explicitly when relevant:

- Why would a buyer refuse to adopt this?
- What is the cheapest substitute?
- What can a competitor reproduce in 30 days?
- Which advantage disappears if the underlying open-source model/library improves?
- Which dependency can destroy margins or availability?
- What must be true for this to become a real business rather than an interesting demo?
- What work should be stopped because it does not increase product or commercial leverage?
